# Proposition V2 (final)

Phone as a key, to access a private survey, for participants.

This prototype treats the questionnaire as a locked safe that must be unlocked. The mobile phone generates a series of physical actions — shake, swipe, or press and hold — and the participants need to complete these actions, just like turning a knob to find the correct combination. Only after completing these actions will the questionnaire link be unlocked. This is not a button click, but an action that requires actual operation, such as dropping the ballot into the ballot box in front of others.

It is this physical interaction that makes this mechanism seem real and credible. The questionnaire is deployed on public terminals, such as in school halls, entrances, or cafeterias. The built-in sensors in the mobile phone can confirm whether the user has actually performed these actions, and only when the actions are completed at the terminal, in front of others, does the unlocking process appear real and reliable.

After the test, the prototype was adjusted. The tester felt that the three actions were completed too quickly and did not create the feeling of unlocking. Therefore, the action sequence was increased to five. The motion sensor request, which was easy to miss partway through, now happens once at the start. In addition, the questionnaire is opened through a real link, rather than a button on the canvas.
